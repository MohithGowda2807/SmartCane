Smart Intelligent Cane
System Architecture and Design
Revision A  |  20 September 2026  |  Status: PROPOSED, NOT VERIFIED
This is the proposed architecture and design for the Smart Intelligent Cane, from the physical level up to the application level. It is a design proposal, not a verified schematic: pin
functions, part numbers and I2C addresses were written from memory and must be checked against datasheets before anyone builds from them.
The cane has two processors. An ESP32-S3 is always on and owns every safety function. A Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W is a switchable gateway for camera, voice AI and cloud.
Contents: 1. Engineering diagram set (architecture, hardware block diagram, schematic sheets 3A-3C)   2. Part 1: hardware connections and pin allocations   3. Part 2: software
architecture, UML and flows   4. Part 3: power distribution and wiring harness   5. Design decisions and open points.
Conventions: TBD = not defined in the project specification. In the schematic, nets with the same name are connected; pin names are inside each IC body and net names are on the
stubs outside; red bold nets are power rails. IC pad numbers are left out on purpose until taken from datasheets. Raspberry Pi pin numbers are physical 40-pin header numbers.

3.8 State machine: system and power modes
The cane drops to POWER_SAVE at 20 % battery and returns to NORMAL at 25 %; the 5 % gap stops it flapping between modes. SOS is reachable from any running mode.
Mode
What runs
NORMAL
Camera, voice AI, cloud, all sensors, haptics; modem on standby
POWER_SAVE
Pi off. Ultrasonic, ToF, IMU and water sensing, haptics, adaptive tip and SOS only
SOS
Modem forced on, SMS and call, beacon, location update every 60 s
CHARGING
Sensors idle; returns to the mode it came from when unplugged
OFF
Entered after a clean Pi shutdown, or at 3 % to protect the cells after a final low-battery alert
The 20 % threshold follows the documentation's 15 to 20 % figure; final values need real battery data.

3.9 Safety loop (ESP32-S3, every 50 ms)
All five checks run every cycle, and the last one is what makes the support legs deploy automatically when the surface changes. If several checks fire in one cycle the alert priority is
fall, then step, then obstacle, then water. The 1.2 m, 8 cm, 15 s and 3 s figures are example thresholds to tune in trials.

4. Part 3: power and wiring
4.1 Diagram 4: power distribution architecture
One single-cell pack (two 18650 cells in parallel, 3.0 to 4.2 V) feeds five rails; only 3V3_MAIN is always on, and the ESP32-S3 switches the rest through the TCA9555. Logic power
(green) is kept separate from high-current modem and motor power (orange).

4.3 Wiring harness
Two keyed connectors let the cane come apart for service: J1 (20 pins) between handle and shaft, and J2 (14 pins) between shaft and tip. The camera is not on J1; it has its own
22-pin CSI-2 FFC from the Pi, kept under 200 mm.

Harness rules
•  Power and ground in 24 AWG; signals in 28 AWG.
•  DSCL and DSDA run as two twisted pairs.
•  The US_ECHO divider sits on the shaft PCB end.
•  Spring-loaded pogo pins, with the ground pins mating first.
•  TVS arrays on every J1 and J2 signal pin for ESD.
•  Strain relief at both joints, sealed against water.
•  Motor wires routed away from the I2S lines.

5. Design decisions and open points
Six decisions in this design are not in the project documentation and each one changes the wiring, so the team should review them before the schematic starts.
Decision
Reason
Headset audio comes from the Pi, not the ESP32-S3
The ESP32-S3 supports Bluetooth Low Energy only, which cannot stream audio to a headset. The ESP-to-Pi link uses the Pi's mini-UART
so the Pi's Bluetooth stays free
All three mics connect to the ESP32-S3
The Pi has one audio input bus (two mics at most); the ESP32-S3 has two I2S peripherals
The modem has two connections: UART to the ESP32-S3, USB to the Pi
SOS must work with the Pi powered off, which POWER_SAVE requires
Single-cell battery (two cells in parallel)
The modem runs straight from the cell and charging stays simple
Tip sensors sit behind a differential I2C buffer
Plain I2C is unreliable over a cable of about 1.1 m
A TCA9555 expander carries slow control signals
The ESP32-S3 module runs out of safe pins
To verify before building
•  Every part number, pin function and I2C address against its datasheet; this document was written from memory.
•  Pi mini-UART RTS/CTS on GPIO16 and 17, and its reliability at the chosen baud rate.
•  ESP32-S3 GPIO3 as the expander interrupt input (it is a strapping pin).
•  Boot-log output on GPIO43 not disturbing the modem.
•  SIM7600G-H UART and PWRKEY logic level against 3.3 V: a level translator is needed unless the breakout board includes one.
•  MAX30102 needs a 1.8 V core supply: confirm the chosen module has its own regulator.
•  Regulator, charger and load-switch choices, and the battery capacity.
•  Thresholds in the state machine and safety loop, tuned in user trials.
•  Which sensors trigger leg deployment; the documentation does not say, and the leg mechanism itself is still a concept.
•  IMU location: the documentation places it in the shaft in one section and the tip in another; this design uses the tip.
•  UWB, NFC and RFID are still options under evaluation; the Pi SPI pins are only reserved for UWB.
•  Braille: the project documentation lists the E button with the L pattern (dots 1-2-3); the correct E is dots 1-5.
